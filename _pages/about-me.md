I am a Computer Science PhD student at Tulane University with a background in software engineering and empirical software engineering research. I work as a graduate research assistant in the [Laboratory for Software Design](https://lab-design.github.io/), advised by [Dr. Hridesh Rajan](https://sse.tulane.edu/cs/faculty/rajan). My current interests center on reliable AI agents and the intersection of artificial intelligence, software engineering, and programming languages.

I came to this area through software engineering and research on software quality. My master's research investigated machine-learning approaches for predicting high-risk technical debt in open-source software projects. That work showed me how much empirical methods can reveal about complex software systems, and how wide the gap often is between what a tool reports and what actually matters to the people maintaining the software.

My current research extends this perspective to AI systems. As AI agents increasingly interact with tools, software repositories, APIs, databases, and other external environments, their reliability becomes a software engineering problem as much as a machine learning one. I am interested in software engineering and programming-language approaches that can help specify, analyze, test, and improve these systems. My current work focuses on the reliability and security of tool-using AI agents.

Before my PhD, I spent several years building software: web, mobile, and AI-enabled systems, both in industry and as a freelancer. I see that experience as complementary to research. It gives me practical insight into how complex systems are designed, implemented, tested, and maintained, and where they tend to break.
