**Status:** ongoing (PhD research, Laboratory for Software Design, Tulane University, 2026–present)

### Motivation

Tool-using AI agents carry out tasks as sequences of steps: they plan, call tools with parameters, read results, and update their state. Each of these steps can go wrong, and because agents act on real systems, those failures can have real consequences. Judging an agent only by its final output misses much of what actually happened along the way.

### What I am working on

My research investigates techniques for improving the reliability and security of AI agents that interact with software tools and external environments. I am approaching the problem from a software engineering and programming-language perspective, treating an agent's trajectory (its tool calls, parameters, intermediate state, and the provenance of the information it acts on) as something that can be specified, tracked, and checked.

### Focus areas

- Agent reliability and safety across multi-step interactions
- Tool-call and parameter-level behavior
- State tracked across long-running interactions
- Failure analysis and systematic evaluation
- Software engineering abstractions (contracts, refinement, provenance) applied to agents

_This is work in progress. Results will be added here once they are published._
